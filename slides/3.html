Warm-up: Beyond the Basics

3–9 min · Level check
B1+

Describe a day that went completely wrong.

B2

Would you rather have more money or more free time? Why?

C1

Is it better to specialize or to be a generalist?

To what extent do you think social media shapes our opinions?

Answer in at least 4 sentences. Give reasons and examples!
Notas · slide 3 (3–9 min)
Comece no B2 e ajuste. Observe: fluidez, uso de conectores, tempos verbais, precisão de vocabulário, se ela desenvolve a resposta sozinha. Faça follow-ups (“Why do you think so?”, “Can you give me an example?”). Anote 3 erros e 3 acertos para o feedback final. Se for fácil, suba para C1; se travar, volte ao B1+ e peça só 2–3 frases.

Giving Your Opinion

9–13 min · Expressions
I tend to think that…Eu costumo achar que…Mais suave que “I think”.
From my perspective…Do meu ponto de vista…From my perspective, remote work is more productive.
As far as I’m concerned…No que me diz respeito…Opinião firme e pessoal.
It seems to me that…Me parece que…Opinião com cautela.
If you ask me…Se você quer saber…Informal e natural.
I’m inclined to believe…Estou inclinada a acreditar…Formal, bom para debates.
Notas · slide 4 (9–13 min)
Escolha um tema (ex.: “Is working from home better?”). Ela dá a opinião usando 3 expressões DIFERENTES, sem repetir “I think”. Mais difícil: ela precisa defender com um exemplo pessoal. Corrija entonação e naturalidade, não só gramática.

Agreeing & Disagreeing

13–17 min · Expressions
AgreeAbsolutely. / I couldn’t agree more.That’s exactly how I see it.
Partly agreeI see your point, but…You’re right up to a point, however…
Disagree politelyI’m afraid I see it differently.I’m not so sure about that.
Challenge an ideaBut doesn’t that mean…?What makes you say that?
ClarifyWhat I mean is…Let me put it another way.
ConcedeFair enough. / Good point.I hadn’t thought of it that way.
Notas · slide 5 (13–17 min)
Mini-debate: você propõe afirmações polêmicas leves (“Cities are better than small towns”, “Everyone should learn to code”). Ela precisa discordar educadamente e depois conceder um ponto. Ensine que discordar em inglês é mais suave do que em português: “I’m afraid…”, “I’m not sure that…”. Nada de “You are wrong”.

Present Perfect vs Past Simple

25–29 min · Advanced grammar
Past SimpleMomento terminado e definidoI visited Rio in 2019.Did you see the news this morning?
Present PerfectExperiência ou ligação com o presenteI’ve visited Rio three times.Have you ever lived abroad?
for / sinceDuração até agoraI’ve worked here for five years.I’ve lived here since 2020.
just / already / yetAções recentesI’ve just finished my report.Haven’t you eaten yet?
Notas · slide 8 (25–29 min)
Regra simples: tempo definido (yesterday, in 2019, last week) = Past Simple; experiência/resultado/“até agora” = Present Perfect. Exercício: “Tell me about something you’ve done that most people haven’t.” Ela conta usando Present Perfect e depois dá detalhes no Past Simple (“I’ve been to Japan. I went in 2022 and…”). Esse movimento perfect → past é o mais natural em inglês.

Conditionals: What If…?

29–33 min · Advanced grammar
Real (1st)If + present, will + verbIf it rains, I’ll stay home.
Unreal (2nd)If + past, would + verbIf I won the lottery, I’d travel the world.
Past regret (3rd)If + had + particípio, would have + particípioIf I had studied more, I would have passed.
If you could change one thing about your past, what would it be?
Notas · slide 9 (29–33 min)
Faça perguntas em cadeia: “What would you do if you won a million dollars?” (2nd), “What would you have done differently last year?” (3rd). Aceite pequenos erros e faça recast. Mais difícil: ela encadeia 3 frases, cada uma nascendo da anterior (“If I won the lottery, I’d quit my job. If I quit, I’d…”). Mais confortável: só 2nd conditional.

Notas · slide 10 (33–36 min)
Desafio: ela responde “Is it better to live in a big city or a small town?” usando pelo menos 3 conectores desta lista. Você marca no ar cada conector usado. Conectores são o que transforma frases soltas em fala estruturada, que é o salto para B2.

Storytelling in 5 Steps

36–40 min · Speaking
1Set the scene: “It was last summer, and I was…”
2The problem: “Suddenly, I realized that…”
3Details: “At first… then… after that…”
4The result: “In the end, we managed to…”
5The lesson: “What I learned is that…”
Tell a real story: a trip, a mistake, an unexpected surprise.
Notas · slide 11 (36–40 min)
Dê 1 minuto para ela pensar e 2 minutos para contar. Você só escuta e faz perguntas de curiosidade no final (“What happened next?”, “How did you feel?”). Observe uso de Past Simple, Past Continuous (“I was walking when…”) e conectores. Não interrompa; anote os erros para o feedback.

Mini Debate: Remote Work

40–44 min · Guided dialogue
Honestly, I think remote work is the future. It saves time and money.
I see your point, but isn’t it harder to stay motivated at home?
Not necessarily. Although it takes discipline, I’m more focused at home.
Fair enough. However, what about building relationships with colleagues?
That’s a good point. Maybe a hybrid model is the best of both worlds.
I couldn’t agree more. If companies offered that, everyone would win.
Notas · slide 12 (40–44 min)
1) Leia o diálogo e peça que ela destaque as expressões de opinião, concessão e conectores. 2) Faça o papel A e ela o B, depois invertam. 3) Improviso: troque o tema (“Should school start later?”, “AI in the classroom”) e ela debate sem ler. Você defende o lado oposto ao dela para forçar argumentação.

30 seconds to prepare. Ready? Go!
Notas · slide 13 (44–47 min)
30 segundos de preparo. Ela fala ~2 min; você marca num papel os itens usados (idiom, phrasal verb, conectores). No fim, responda a pergunta dela e faça um follow-up. Meta: fluência com estrutura, não perfeição. Mais difícil: sem pausa de preparo.

I like very much travel.→ I’m really into traveling.Expressão natural.
It’s very very important.→ It’s absolutely crucial.Adjetivos fortes.
I have 3 years working here.→ I’ve been working here for 3 years.Present Perfect Continuous.
Notas · slide 14 (47–49 min)
Comece por 3 acertos reais (ótimo uso de conectores, boa história, etc.). Depois use 2–3 erros REAIS que você anotou; os exemplos do slide são erros comuns de brasileiros. Mostre o “upgrade”: a frase não estava errada, mas pode soar mais natural. Ela repete a versão melhorada.
